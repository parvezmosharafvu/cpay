Security and recovery

Beta. CPAY Wallet is new. Only use small amounts you can afford to lose while it is being tested.

Who holds the keys

You do. The wallet creates a 12-word recovery phrase on your phone. The keys that control your money come from those words and are made on your phone. The phrase is stored on the phone only, encrypted with your passphrase. It is never sent to CPAY or to anyone else.

CPAY cannot see your recovery phrase or passphrase, cannot move the money in this wallet, and cannot recover it for you.

How payments work

The wallet uses the Spark network, a Bitcoin layer, through an open-source wallet library (the Breez SDK). Lightning payments go through a Spark service provider.

  • Every transfer needs a signature from the keys on your phone. The Spark operators (currently Lightspark, Breez and Flashnet; two of the three must co-sign) cannot move your money without that signature.
  • Spark's safety relies on at least one operator deleting old key shares as designed. If the operators stop working, Spark lets you exit to the Bitcoin blockchain on your own, with an on-chain fee. This version can save an exit backup (Security → Save exit backup): the pre-signed transactions needed for that exit. It does not have an exit button yet; CPAY will publish exit steps before any operator shutdown affects users.
  • While the operators are working, sending and receiving depend on them and on the Spark service provider. Holding your own keys means nobody can move the money without your phone's signature; it does not mean the wallet works without Spark.
  • The wallet library keeps its own records on your phone. CPAY builds it with the library's real-time sync service turned off, so wallet records are not copied to that service. The wallet needs an internet connection to the Spark network to pay or be paid. It does not need the CPAY website.

Your recovery phrase

  • Write the 12 words on paper, in order. Keep it private and safe from fire and water. Do not photograph it or store it in the cloud.
  • Anyone with the words can take the money. CPAY staff will never ask for them. Never type them into a website, a form, a chat or an email.
  • Your passphrase only unlocks the wallet on this phone. It is not a backup: without the 12 words, a lost or reset phone means the money is gone.

Recover on a new phone

  1. Open CPAY Wallet on the new phone (from the CPAY website's Download wallet page).
  2. Choose Restore with a recovery phrase and type the 12 words.
  3. Choose a new passphrase. Your balance and history appear once the wallet has synced.

Restoring in other apps built on the same library may work, but CPAY has not tested that and does not promise it.

Keeping this phone's copy safe

  • The wallet locks itself after 5 minutes without use. Use the Lock button when you hand the phone to someone.
  • Install it to the home screen. A browser that is not installed may delete website data it has not seen for a while; the recovery phrase on paper is what brings the wallet back.
  • Clearing the browser's site data, or "Remove wallet from this phone", deletes this phone's copy. The money is safe only if you have the 12 words.

Your CPAY website account is separate

Your CPAY website balance (deposits, earnings, USDT withdrawals) is held and recorded by CPAY. That does not change. This wallet does not hold it, and the website does not hold this wallet's money.

Linking your CPAY account in the wallet is optional. It signs the app in to CPAY so it can show the website balance, labelled as held by CPAY. It never sends the recovery phrase or keys, and it moves no money. Unlink signs the app out; Unlink and sign out everywhere also ends your other CPAY sign-ins.
