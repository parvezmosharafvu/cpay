How it works

From payment link to payment received

Your customer can pay without opening a CPAY account.

  1. Create a payment link

    Give your link a name, choose a checkout design and share it with your customer.

  2. Your customer pays

    They enter an amount in US dollars and pay the Lightning invoice. Your desk shows the payment status.

  3. Receive the net amount

    The platform fee is deducted first. Auto withdraw forwards the rest to your saved address; with it off, the rest goes to your CPAY balance.

Receiving options

Choose how you receive your money

Your Auto withdraw setting decides what happens after a payment settles.

Auto withdraw on

To your saved address

The net amount is forwarded to your saved Lightning or supported USDT address. If it cannot be sent immediately, it stays held or owed and is retried.

Set up your account
Auto withdraw off

Keep a CPAY balance

The net amount goes to your balance held by CPAY. Choose when to withdraw from your desk, review the quote and confirm with your passkey.

Security

Protection around every payout

Passkey sign-in

Add a passkey from the Security page of your desk and sign in with your fingerprint, face or screen lock.

Confirm before a payout

A withdrawal asks you to confirm it is you again when your sign-in is not recent.

Sign in again to change it

Saving or changing a receive address needs your passkey. You get a notice when it changes.

One credit, one send

A payment is credited once and a withdrawal is sent once, even if a button is pressed twice.

Custody

Where your money is held

Your CPAY account

With Auto withdraw off, your balance is held by CPAY. Withdraw it from your desk with passkey confirmation. With Auto withdraw on, the net amount is forwarded to your saved address; amounts waiting to be sent remain owed to you.

CPAY Wallet, a separate app

CPAY Wallet, when it is released, is a separate app: its keys stay on your phone and CPAY cannot move or recover money in it. Your CPAY balance is not kept in that app, and nothing moves between the two.
